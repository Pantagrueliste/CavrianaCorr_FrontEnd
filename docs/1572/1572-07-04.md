<div class="letter-summary">

A rumour is circulating at the French court that the Florentines have lent the Duke of Alba three, or by other accounts eight, hundred thousand scudi, and that he has kept his footing against the exiles by this money. Cavriana warns that the accusation is being used by his correspondents' adversaries to discredit them with the French crown. He reports the debate over whether to take up arms in Flanders — pressed by the Huguenots alone, resisted by the king and the queen mother — and the imminent arrival of Navarre and Condé for a wedding that will be celebrated very plainly. The arrival of the Duke of Ferrara's agent, called Gianetta, can only cause trouble; Cavriana will set his hounds to watch what is negotiated.
